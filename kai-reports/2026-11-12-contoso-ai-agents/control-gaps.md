# Control gap analysis — contoso-simulation (Contoso AI agents in Copilot Studio and Microsoft Foundry, SC-500 lab)
Frameworks: NIST CSF 2.0 (Feb 2024) · ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A · NIST AI RMF 1.0 (AI 100-1, Jan 2023) · ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Annex A

Notes on this mapping
- Input: `evidence.md` (E-01 to E-70 and its Gaps section). Rows cite evidence IDs. See **Risk cross-reference** below for the mapping from systemic gaps to R-IDs in `risk-register.csv` (added after auditor review X-08).
- All facts come from a simulated model of a tenant (evidence.md scope check, `json:3`). No policy, procedure or process documents were in the evidence. So a gap type says what the *evidence* shows. It does not confirm that Contoso has no written control.
- Labels (steering rule 2): the evidence IDs are the *Observations*. The "Gap statement" column is *Risk* (judgement). "Suggested control improvements" are *Recommendations*.
- Control IDs: I cite an ID only where I am confident of it. Where I am not, the control area is named and marked `(verify ID)`. For ISO/IEC 42001 I cite clause groups (A.3, A.4, A.6, A.10) as certain and mark every sub-control number `(verify ID)`. For access control and secrets, NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 have no specific control. ISO/IEC 27001 carries those rows, as 42001 expects.
- Gap types: **Design** = no control is evidenced, or the evidenced design allows the condition. **Operating** = a control or check is evidenced but did not produce the right outcome. **Evidence** = the evidence cannot show whether there is a gap.

## Systemic gaps
| # | Missing or weak control | Findings affected | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **AI agent inventory and accountable ownership.** The inventory records one owner per agent and nothing else. It has no backup owner or sponsor. It records no owner for blueprints, agent identities, managed identities or maker connections. It has no positive "owner active" status. The orphan check fires, but the orphaned agent is still Published. | E-01, E-02, E-04, E-12, E-20, E-28, E-36, E-41, E-69; E-15, E-44, E-45, E-51, E-52; Gaps: "Ownership process" | Nobody is answerable for 5 published agents and 8 non-human identities, blueprints and connections (4 agent identities, 1 managed identity, 2 blueprints, 1 maker connection) that hold tenant-wide and production access. This is the root gap: S2 to S5 all need a named owner to act. |
| S2 | **Non-human identity lifecycle (joiner/mover/leaver, decommissioning, recertification).** Two agents run without a dedicated identity, and one of them uses a person's maker credentials. Two agent identities hold standing tenant-wide grants but have no agent. All 5 agents are 49 to 255 days idle and still Published. No access review is evidenced. | E-06, E-07, E-38, E-52; E-44, E-45; E-01, E-09, E-17, E-25, E-33, E-40; Gaps: "Access reviews", "Activity detail" | Identities and agents outlive their purpose and keep their access. A maker-credential connection ties HR-Confidential access to one employee's account and lifecycle. |
| S3 | **Privileged access and least privilege for agent identities.** Grants are inherited from blueprints at tenant scope: Mail.Read on all mailboxes and Sites.Read.All on all sites. One blueprint credential can act as 3 identities. Agent identities hold Contributor on rg-production, Key Vault Secrets User and the Helpdesk Administrator role. No privileged-access standard or approval is evidenced for any of these. | E-18, E-26, E-34, E-42, E-43, E-46, E-47, E-48, E-50, E-55, E-56, E-57 | The blast-radius scores of agent-devops (74) and agent-invoice (35, or 39 if the blueprint leaks) come from standing privilege. | S4 | **Secrets management and trust boundaries.** The finance blueprint client secret is stored in kv-contoso-ops. The DevOps managed identity can read that vault. This creates a 7-hop path from a developer workstation to all mailboxes and all sites. Rotation, expiry and vault access policy are not evidenced. Where the bp-itops credential is stored is not evidenced. | E-48, E-49, E-51, E-53, E-56, E-64, E-65, E-66; Gaps: "Secret details", "bp-itops credential" | One secret is a choke point on 4 of 11 attack paths (E-66). | S5 | **Untrusted and third-party input handling, and a pre-publication gate.** One agent is published anonymously to the internet with a Highly Confidential knowledge source. One agent processes external email with tenant-wide read grants. One agent with password-reset power is open to any employee. No guardrails, human-in-the-loop step, DLP or environment policy is evidenced. | E-05, E-08, E-10, E-13, E-29, E-31, E-35, E-61, E-63; Gaps: "Guardrails", "Environment governance" | The shortest attack path is 2 hops from the anonymous internet to HR-Confidential data (E-63). No evidenced control stops an agent from being published with this exposure. |
| S6 | **Monitoring of agent activity, and validation of the exposure tooling.** No logging, alerting, transcript retention or prompt-injection detection is evidenced. The analyzer that governance would rely on has blind spots: a fixed inactivity date, a privilege filter that skips Key Vault Secrets User, and a top-5 truncation that hides a credential choke point. | E-26, E-66, E-68, E-70; Gaps: "Logging / monitoring", "Tool inactivity cutoff" | Without monitoring, abuse of S3 to S5 would not be detected. Without validated tooling, the inventory under-reports risk to the people who own it. |

## Suggested control improvements
Owners are not evidenced. Each line carries a suggested owner per steering rule 5.

- **S1 Inventory and ownership:** The AI governance lead keeps one AI agent register. It covers every agent and its dependent identities, blueprints, connections, tools and credentials. Each entry names a primary and a backup owner. The register is reconciled against Defender XDR / Entra inventory **monthly**. *Owner: TBD (suggest: Head of AI Governance).*
- **S1 Orphan response:** The agent's platform admin suspends, or reassigns to a new owner, any agent whose owners are all disabled, **within 5 business days of the orphan flag**. *Owner: TBD (suggest: Power Platform / Foundry platform owner).*
- **S2 Leaver:** HR offboarding raises a task to transfer or retire all agents, connections and agent identities owned by the leaver **at each termination**. IAM confirms closure before the account is disabled. *Owner: TBD (suggest: IAM manager).*
- **S2 Identity standard:** Platform admins block publication of any agent that runs on maker or personal credentials or lacks a dedicated Entra agent or managed identity. They check this **at every publish**. *Owner: TBD (suggest: IAM manager).*
- **S2 Recertification and dormancy:** Agent owners recertify their agent's identities, grants and audience **quarterly**. IAM removes grants from identities with no owning agent or no activity for 90 days, measured from the review date. *Owner: TBD (suggest: IAM manager).*
- **S3 Least privilege:** The IAM manager approves every application permission, Azure RBAC role and Entra directory role for an agent identity **before assignment**. Approval requires a scoped alternative to be documented, such as an Exchange application access policy for invoices@ or Sites.Selected. Tenant-wide grants inherited from blueprints are reviewed **quarterly**. *Owner: TBD (suggest: IAM manager).*
- **S3 Production privilege:** Cloud platform owners give agent identities write roles on production through just-in-time elevation with approval only. Standing Contributor is reviewed **monthly** until it is removed. *Owner: TBD (suggest: Cloud Platform Engineering lead).*
- **S4 Secrets:** The Key Vault owner stores agent and blueprint credentials in a vault per business domain. Read access is limited to that domain's identities. Each credential has an expiry of 180 days or less and is rotated **on schedule and on any owner change**. Credential-free options (certificates or federated credentials) are preferred where the platform supports them. *Owner: TBD (suggest: Cloud Security / Key Vault owner).*
- **S5 Pre-publication gate:** The AI governance lead approves every agent **before it is published or its channel changes**. The approval records authentication mode, audience, knowledge-source sensitivity, untrusted input channels (public web, external email, writable repositories) and the guardrails applied. An unauthenticated channel with Confidential or higher knowledge is rejected. *Owner: TBD (suggest: Head of AI Governance).*
- **S5 Untrusted input:** Agent owners whose agents take external input (email, public web) test for indirect prompt injection and require human approval for privileged tool calls. They do this **before publishing and after each material change**. *Owner: TBD (suggest: AppSec lead).*
- **S6 Monitoring:** The SOC enables and retains agent activity, tool-call and sign-in logs for every published agent and agent identity. It alerts on agent use of privileged tools (password reset, Key Vault get secret, Run command on VM, tenant-wide mail reads). Coverage is checked against the S1 register **monthly**. *Owner: TBD (suggest: SOC manager).*
- **S6 Tool validation:** The exposure-management owner validates analyzer rules against the register **quarterly**. Rules to check: inactivity relative to the run date, the privilege-filter scope including Key Vault data-plane roles, and full choke-point lists, not top-N only. *Owner: TBD (suggest: Exposure Management lead).*
